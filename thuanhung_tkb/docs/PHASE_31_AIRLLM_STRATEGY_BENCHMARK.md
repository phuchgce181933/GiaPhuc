## How this benchmark works

Two questions, kept apart on purpose:

1. **Tier A — does the AirLLM service work?** Health, readiness, and one `/plan` call
   whose decision the Node validator accepts. It establishes a runtime, nothing else.
2. **Tier B — is the AirLLM strategy useful?** The deterministic fallback and AirLLM
   are compared on one fixed SchedulingInput.

A Tier A PASS is not evidence for Tier B, and neither is reported as the other.

### What is held constant (and how you can check)

| Held constant | Where it is enforced |
|---|---|
| SchedulingInput | `benchmarkInputHash` over a personal-data-free structural projection; the input is never mutated between runs |
| solver seed, per-solve and overall time budgets, `respectStrategyMode`, `requireFeasibility` | one frozen `BENCHMARK_SOLVER_PROFILE`; every run records the options it received, and both arms' records are compared for equality |
| candidate count | the MAXIMUM any arm asked for, decided before any solve |
| solver implementation, independent evaluator, Phase 28 scorer | the same functions production calls; the benchmark reads their output and computes no opinion of its own |

### What the AI is allowed to do

Strategy selection, weight suggestion, candidate count, rationale. Nothing else. It cannot
name a teacher, a class, a day, a period, a branch, or a slot: the decision vocabulary is a
closed set, and a field the validator does not recognise is a rejection of the whole
decision rather than a silent no-op. A decision that fails validation falls back, and the
fallback run is never scored as if it were an AI run.

### How the conclusion is reached

Policy `AIRLLM_EVAL_POLICY_V1`, decided on `bestGlobalScore` alone, aggregated by median,
epsilon `0.000001`. Validity gates the verdict. When the two arms' ranges overlap, a
BETTER or WORSE verdict describes the median only and the conclusion is downgraded.

Two yardsticks are computed and both are shown: the per-run pool-relative score the policy
decides on, and a shared-pool score that puts both arms' shipped schedules on one common
scale. If they contradict, no direction is claimed.

Only these five conclusions are reachable, and the renderer refuses to print any other:

- `AIRLLM_RUNTIME_READY`
- `AI_STRATEGY_VALID_BUT_NO_MEASURABLE_GAIN`
- `AI_STRATEGY_IMPROVES_RESULTS`
- `AI_STRATEGY_WORSE_THAN_FALLBACK`
- `AIRLLM_BENCHMARK_BLOCKED`

A measurement that could not be taken is reported as not taken. Nothing here is retried
until the numbers look better, and no run is selected out of the set.

### Shared-pool re-scoring

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| candidates in the shared pool | `10` |
| scored | `10` |
| runs with no schedule to score | `0` |


Phase 28 normalizes each dimension against its own pool's min and max, so a total is a position within a pool, not an absolute quality. These scores share ONE pool across both arms, which is what makes them comparable to each other.

`sharedPoolGlobalScore` uses the scorer's own default weights, not either arm's
strategy weights. The AI's weights are a legitimate part of its strategy, but they are
not the yardstick — one fixed yardstick is applied to both arms' shipped schedules.
